The revised plan fixes what was wrong with the initial one:

- **Scope:** Only the customer progress screen changes. The staff/store portal stays at 15s, so its expensive list and progress calls are not multiplied.
- **Backend:** No backend changes. The route has no provider dispatch, and auth, session, schema and signing scopes are untouched.
- **Load:** About 312 requests/min (5.2 rps) is a worst-case ceiling, against a real volume of a handful per day. The 10-minute idle step-down to 30s holds a forgotten tab to 2 requests/min. Dropping the 72-attempt hard stop is then reasonable, because unchanged session expiry ends any forgotten tab with a 401.
- **Retries and errors:** Retryable codes, permanent codes and auth expiry are each handled clearly. Honoring Retry-After even on manual refresh prevents users from hammering through a throttle.
- **Timer lifecycle:** It uses one timer and the existing busy guard. It has an exact visibility condition, and handles hidden, offline, pagehide and BFcache without creating duplicate timers.

None of these notes blocks approval, but cover them during implementation:

1. **Idempotency of the polled call:** The route is a POST that runs "finalize/read". Add a test that repeated 5s polls after completion produce no extra writes, timestamps or audit rows.
2. **Retry-After cap:** Limit very large or invalid Retry-After values (for example, at most 5 minutes), and treat a past or unparseable date as the normal backoff.
3. **Duplicate tabs:** The load estimate assumes one tab per role, but nothing enforces it. That's fine at current volume; just note it in the rollout doc.
4. **Polling after completion:** Confirm that continuing at 30s after completion is only for showing pending corrections. If nothing can change after completion, stop there.
